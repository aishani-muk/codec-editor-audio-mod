Streaming codec-token editor for raga and Celtic music

A/B demo combining both research arms in one page. Each clip plays at 24 kHz mono from pipeline/results/ WAVs decoded by the same WavTokenizer + cosine-crossfade overlap-add streaming pipeline. The Saraga DSP demo shows the deterministic baseline; the Celtic Track A demo shows the trained ML editor.

3 raga clips × 2 u(t) profiles. The DSP back-end (parametric EQ + compressor + pitch shift, all conditioned on u(t)) runs through the streaming pipeline at RTF < 0.12. The trained ML editor (editor_v3_lora) is u-blind on this corpus and is deferred to the metrics table below.

4. What this demo establishes (and what it doesn't)

Three failure modes, one infrastructure
  • editor_v3_lora: doesn't edit at all. Bit-identical MD5s across u ∈ {0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9}. Likely cause: T5 collapses the "intensity 0.x" suffix to nearly identical embeddings.
  • editor_v3: edits in the correct direction, breaks raga structure. ΔA = −1.35 (calms), but drift = 292 cents (3 semitones — a different raga to a listener) and JSD = 0.38 (Yaman becomes Yaman-shaped noise).
  • celtic_track_a on Saraga: edits in the wrong direction. Same architecture as editor_v3, trained on Celtic — produces ΔA = +0.47 on raga (agitates) but ΔA = −1.55 on Celtic (calms). Direction flips across domains.
  • DSP baseline on Saraga: also wrong direction (mild). Likely M2E Western-pop bias on Hindustani vocal.
